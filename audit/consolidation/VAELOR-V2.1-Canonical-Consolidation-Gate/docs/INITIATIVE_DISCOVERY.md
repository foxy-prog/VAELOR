# VÆLOR V2.1 — Initiative Discovery Engine

## Purpose

The Initiative Discovery Engine converts evaluated opportunities into structured initiative candidates aligned with the operator's goals, commitments, resources, constraints, and strategic direction.

It does not independently commit the operator.

## Core distinction

```text
OPPORTUNITY = something potentially worth pursuing
INITIATIVE  = a structured proposal for pursuing it
COMMITMENT  = an authorized decision to pursue it
MISSION     = an executable objective created from that commitment
```

## Pipeline

```text
OPPORTUNITY
    ↓
ASSESS
    ↓
GOAL ALIGNMENT
    ↓
RESOURCE CHECK
    ↓
CONFLICT CHECK
    ↓
RISK CHECK
    ↓
INITIATIVE CANDIDATE
    ↓
STRATEGIC EVALUATION
    ↓
RECOMMENDATION
    ↓
DECISION BOUNDARY
    ↓
REJECT / WATCH / EXPLORE / COMMIT
```

## Initiative candidate

A candidate records:
- originating opportunity
- strategic objective
- expected outcome
- rationale
- required resources
- estimated effort
- dependencies
- conflicts
- risks
- expected value
- confidence
- success criteria
- verification criteria
- recommended decision
- expiry/review date

## Commitment boundary

The engine cannot:
- create external commitments
- change strategic goals
- allocate consequential resources
- launch missions
- grant authority
- bypass governance

without the applicable authorization.

## Strategic alignment

An initiative should be evaluated against:
- current goals
- active commitments
- project portfolio
- constraints
- opportunity cost
- risk tolerance
- available resources
- deadlines
- long-term strategic direction

## Portfolio awareness

Initiatives must be evaluated against existing work. A good idea may still be rejected because it creates unacceptable overload, dependency conflicts, or opportunity cost.

## Canonical rule

> VÆLOR may discover and formulate what could become the next mission, but only governance and authorized decision-making can turn a proposal into a commitment.
